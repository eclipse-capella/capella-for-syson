= OCB — Operational Capabilities Blank
:page-feature-status: experimental

In the application, this diagram is labeled `OCB - Operational Capability Blank`.

== Purpose

Use an OCB to describe the operational capabilities expected by stakeholders and the participants involved in them.
It answers the question: which operational goals matter, and who is involved in achieving them?

.Operational capabilities of the In-Flight Entertainment System sample model
image::ocb-ife.png[OCB showing the involvement of Passenger and Aircraft in Entertain During Flight, and of Airline Company in Implement a Commercial Strategy]

== Position in Arcadia

Operational Analysis, before defining the system boundary.
The xref:features/oab.adoc[OAB] provides the complementary operational context.

== Main concepts

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Concept |Role
|Operational Capability |A goal or ability expected in the operational situation.
|Operational Entity or Actor |A participant involved in the capability.
|Involvement |The relationship from a capability to an involved participant.
|Capability generalization |A relationship between a specialized capability and a more general capability.
|Requirement and Describes link |A requirement and its relationship to a represented element.
|===

== Create the diagram

Use the *OCB* shortcut in the xref:user-manual:features/editor.adoc[Capella project welcome area], or create an OCB from the explorer on the `Capabilities` package or one of its sub-packages, in Operational Analysis.

xref:user-manual:concepts.adoc#synchronization[Synchronization]: Capabilities, entities, actors, and requirements are unsynchronized; Involvements, Generalizations, Communication Means, and Describes links between displayed elements are synchronized.

== Describe capabilities and involvement

. Use *New Operational Capability* on the background and name the capability for the operational goal.
. Create participants with *New Operational Entity* or *New Operational Actor*, or show existing participants from the explorer.
. From a capability, use *New Involvement* to connect it to a participant.
. Repeat the involvement operation for each participant concerned.

Use *New Generalization* between capabilities when a specialization relationship is needed.
Capability and participant names identify model elements that can be reused in other views.

{product} enforces the following rules, at creation and at reconnection:

* An Involvement connects a capability to an Operational Entity or an Operational Actor.
* A Generalization connects two distinct capabilities.
* A reconnection that would duplicate an existing Involvement or Generalization is refused.

== Describe participants and their communication

* Use *New Operational Entity* or *New Operational Actor* on a participant to create a nested participant.
* Use *New Communication Mean* to connect two participants; both ends of a Communication Mean can be reconnected.
* Drop a participant on another participant to nest it in the model, or on the diagram background to move it back to the Structure package.
