= Arcadia glossary

This glossary defines the Arcadia concepts used in {product}.
The concepts are defined in SysML v2 by the xref:features/arcadia-library.adoc[Arcadia library].
For the general SysML v2 and SysON terms, refer to the https://doc.mbse-syson.org/syson/main/user-manual/concepts.html[SysON key concepts].

Several concepts take a different name depending on the engineering perspective.
The table below gives these names; the definitions follow in alphabetical order.

[glossary]
== Definitions

[[actor]]Actor::
An external entity, such as a user, an operator, or another system, that interacts directly with the system under study.
In {product}, an actor is a component with the *Is Actor* property set; the *Is Human* property identifies human actors.

[[allocation]]Allocation::
A relationship that assigns an element to the element in charge of it, such as a function to the component or actor that performs it.
See xref:concepts.adoc#model[Allocations and realizations].

[[capability]]Capability::
The ability of the system to provide a service that supports high-level operational goals.
It is described in System Analysis, for example in an xref:features/mcb.adoc[MCB].

[[capability-realization]]Capability Realization::
The equivalent of a Capability in the Logical and Physical Architectures.

[[communication-mean]]Communication Mean::
The medium linking Operational Entities and Operational Actors, which carries their Interactions.
It is the Operational Analysis equivalent of a Component Exchange.

[[component]]Component::
A constituent part of the system or of an actor, which contributes to its behavior by performing functions and interacting with other components.

[[component-exchange]]Component Exchange::
A connection between two components, through their Component Ports.
A Component Exchange carries the Functional Exchanges between the functions allocated to these components.

[[component-port]]Component Port::
An interaction point of a component, characterizing what the component needs and what it provides.
Its direction is `in`, `out`, or `inout`.

[[configuration-item]]Configuration Item::
A part of the system, designed, produced, or purchased, and assembled with others to build the system.
Configuration Items are described in the EPBS Architecture.

[[describes]]Describes link::
The relationship between a requirement and the model element it applies to.

[[epbs]]EPBS — End Product Breakdown Structure::
The engineering perspective that organizes the system into the Configuration Items to produce.

[[exchange-item]]Exchange Item::
A set of data, material, or energy that is semantically coherent for its use in a given context, and is carried by exchanges.

[[function]]Function::
An action, an operation, or a service performed by a component or an actor.
A function receives inputs and delivers outputs through its Function Ports, and can be broken down into sub-functions.

[[function-port]]Function Port::
An input or output of a function.
An input port describes an expected input; an output port describes an available output.

[[functional-chain]]Functional Chain::
A grouping of Functional Exchanges and their endpoint functions that describes an expected behavior of the system in a given context.
In {product}, a chain references the selected exchanges; the involved functions are derived from their endpoints.
The sequencing and non-functional property mechanisms of Capella Desktop functional chains are not yet available.

[[functional-exchange]]Functional Exchange::
A flow between two functions, connecting an output Function Port to an input Function Port and carrying Exchange Items.

[[interaction]]Interaction::
An exchange between Operational Activities.
It is the Operational Analysis equivalent of a Functional Exchange.

[[involvement]]Involvement::
The relationship from a capability to the entities or actors that take part in it.

[[logical-architecture]]Logical Architecture::
The engineering perspective that defines how the system works as a white box: its logical components, their functions, and their relationships, independently of technological choices.

[[logical-component]]Logical Component::
A component of the Logical Architecture, which decomposes the system independently of any technological solution.

[[logical-function]]Logical Function::
A function of the Logical Architecture.
It usually realizes one or several System Functions.

[[mission]]Mission::
A purpose the system is tasked with.
A mission generally exploits several capabilities.

[[operational-activity]]Operational Activity::
A step of a process, performed by an Operational Entity or an Operational Actor to achieve an objective.

[[operational-actor]]Operational Actor::
An Operational Entity, usually human, associated with a human resource.

[[operational-analysis]]Operational Analysis::
The engineering perspective that describes what the users of the system need to accomplish, independently of the system itself.

[[operational-capability]]Operational Capability::
An ability expected in the operational situation, involving Operational Entities and Operational Actors.

[[operational-entity]]Operational Entity::
A real-world entity, such as an organization, a group, or another system, that takes part in the operational situation.

[[operational-process]]Operational Process::
A grouping of Interactions and their endpoint Operational Activities that describes an operational behavior.
It is the Operational Analysis equivalent of a Functional Chain.
In {product}, a process references the selected Interactions; the involved activities are derived from their endpoints, without sequencing information.

[[perspective]]Perspective::
One of the Arcadia engineering levels: Operational Analysis, System Analysis, Logical Architecture, Physical Architecture, and EPBS Architecture.
Elements of different perspectives are distinct model elements, even when they have the same name.

[[physical-architecture]]Physical Architecture::
The engineering perspective that defines the final architecture of the system, including its implementation and the resources hosting its behavior.

[[physical-component]]Physical Component::
A component of the Physical Architecture.
In Arcadia, a behavior component implements functions, and a node component provides the resources that host behavior components.
The distinction between behavior and node components, and the deployment of behavior components on node components, are not yet provided by the dedicated Arcadia modeling tools in {product}.

[[physical-function]]Physical Function::
A function of the Physical Architecture.
It usually realizes one or several Logical Functions.

[[realization]]Realization::
A relationship between an element and the element of the previous perspective it refines, such as a System Function realizing an Operational Activity.

[[requirement]]Requirement::
A statement of need attached to model elements through Describes links.

[[system]]System::
The object under study, an organized set of elements functioning as a unit.
In {product}, it is the `system` component of the System Analysis, Logical Architecture, and Physical Architecture `Structure` packages.

[[system-analysis]]System Analysis::
The engineering perspective that defines what the system must do for its users: its functions, its exchanges with actors, and its capabilities, with the system seen as a black box.

[[system-function]]System Function::
A function of the System Analysis.
It usually realizes one or several Operational Activities.
